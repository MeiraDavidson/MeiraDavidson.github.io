The Light That Stays

12. Five Ways to Stay Out of Trouble

I want to share five simple guidelines for living. In Buddhism they’re called the Five Precepts, but don’t let that word scare you off — these aren’t a religious thing. Some version of every one of them shows up in just about every culture, family, and circle of friends on Earth. They’re just smart. They keep your life clean enough that your mind can actually be calm. (It’s very hard to meditate peacefully when you’ve made a mess you’re hiding.)

  1. Protect life; don’t harm living things. Be gentle with people, with animals, with the small creatures most people ignore. Cruelty makes the heart heavy. Kindness makes it light.
  2. Don’t take what isn’t yours. Not things, not credit, not someone else’s turn. Take only what’s truly given.
  3. Be honest. Tell the truth. Lies are exhausting to carry — you have to remember them and guard them forever. The truth is light. You can set it down.
  4. Treat your body and other people with respect. Your body is yours; other people’s bodies are theirs. Keep your friendships and relationships honest and kind, never using people or letting yourself be used. Respect goes both ways.
  5. Keep your mind clear — no alcohol, no drugs, nothing that fogs your thinking. This one matters more than it might sound at twelve, and it’ll matter more as you get older. Alcohol, vapes, weed, pills offered at a party, anything sold to you as a way to “feel better fast” — they all blur the very mind we’ve spent this whole book trying to wake up. They cloud your judgment exactly when you most need it sharp, and they quietly damage the only body you’ll ever get. Saying no isn’t being uptight or boring. It is an act of self-respect — of treating your own life as the precious, irreplaceable thing it is. A clear mind is the whole point. Don’t trade it away cheaply.

Read them again. Not one of them is about belief. Every one of them is about staying free — free of guilt, free of messes, free to sit down at the end of the day with a quiet heart.

Questions to talk about

No answer key. These are for arguing over, with someone who read the same chapter.

  1. He says it is very hard to meditate peacefully when you have made a mess you are hiding. What is the connection between how you live and whether your mind will settle?
  2. Not one of the five is about belief; every one is about staying free — of guilt, of messes. Pick the one you find hardest and say honestly why.
  3. On keeping a clear mind, he writes that saying no is an act of self-respect, not being uptight. How would you say that to a friend without sounding like a lecture?
