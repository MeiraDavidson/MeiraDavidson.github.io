The Light That Stays

1. There’s a Light in You, and It Doesn’t Go Out

Right now, while you read this, something is reading.

Not your eyes — your eyes are just windows. Something behind your eyes is taking in these words and knowing them. That something has been with you your whole life. It was there when you were three years old and afraid of the dark. It’s here now. It will be here tonight when you close your eyes.

We’re going to spend this whole book getting to know that something. And here’s the first surprise: it isn’t a cold, blank, empty quiet. It’s the most alive part of you — wide awake, and, strange as it sounds, quietly glad. People who’ve spent their lives looking for it come back saying the same odd thing: that deep down, underneath all the noise, there’s a kind of brightness. Not a brightness you could ever see — “bright” is just the closest word we have, the way we call a person “bright” when we mean wide awake and clear. It’s really more of a feeling: a quiet joy, the gladness of being awake for no reason at all.

The wonderful secret is this: you already own it. Nobody can take it. You don’t have to earn it or buy it or be good enough for it. It’s just there, waiting, like a sun that’s always shining even when clouds roll across it.

Now — a lot of books will tell you meditation is about getting calm. Calm is good, and we’ll practice it. But calm isn’t the treasure. Calm is just how you get the door open. Once it’s quiet enough inside, you start to notice the light that was there the whole time. That light — and the joy in it — is what this book is really about.

Meditation is simply how we walk back toward it.

This book will teach you how. We’ll start with stories — including one about a kid who was completely lost and found his way — and then I’ll hand you a small toolkit of things you can actually do, some of them in less than a minute, quietly, with nobody around you even noticing.

You don’t have to believe anything to begin. You just have to be a little curious.

Questions to talk about

No answer key. These are for arguing over, with someone who read the same chapter.

  1. The author says calm is not the treasure — it is just how you get the door open. What do you think is behind the door, in his telling?
  2. He insists you already own this and cannot earn or lose it. Why might that be a surprising thing to hear? What are most things we value not like that?
  3. 'You don't have to believe anything to begin. You just have to be a little curious.' Is curiosity enough to start something? What has curiosity alone been enough for in your own life?
