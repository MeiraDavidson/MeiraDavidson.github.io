The Light That Stays

6. The Big Question, and the Surprise Behind It

This is the most important chapter in the book. It starts with a riddle and ends with a surprise. Stay with me.

There’s an old, old teaching from a text called the Shurangama Sutra. In it, a teacher walks a confused student through one simple, mind-bending point. I’ll put it in everyday words:

Everything you can see, hear, feel, or think about — none of that is the real you. The real you is the one doing the seeing, the hearing, the feeling, the thinking.

Let’s test it on yourself, right now.

  • Look at your hand. You can see your hand — so your hand is a thing you see. It’s not the seer. The seer is behind your eyes, watching the hand.
  • Now notice a sound in the room. The sound is a thing you hear. You are the one hearing it.
  • Now notice a thought floating by — maybe “this is weird” or “I’m hungry.” That thought is a thing you noticed. So you can’t be the thought. You’re the one who caught it going by.

Do you feel the trick of it? Everything you can point to — your body, your feelings, your thoughts, the whole world — is something you’re aware of. So none of it can be the awareness itself. You are the awareness. The one watching. The one who was here before the thought arrived and is still here after it leaves.

Most books stop right here. This one keeps going — because finding the watcher is only half the discovery. The other half is finding out what the watcher is like. And it’s not what almost everyone expects.

The Surprise

You might guess that this pure awareness, this “watcher,” is a cold, blank, empty kind of quiet. A nothing. An empty room with the lights off.

It’s the opposite. When you stop chasing thoughts and actually rest as that awareness — not think about it, just sit in it — three things show up. They’re hard to say in words, so I’ll give you pictures.

One — you look for it, and you can’t find a thing. (That’s the first clue, not a problem.) Try to find the watcher. Where is it? What color? How big? You come up empty — there’s no object there to grab, no shape, no edges, no weight. The old teachers called this being “empty.” But careful: not empty like a sad, dark nothing. Empty like the open sky — empty the way a room is empty when there’s space for anything to happen in it.

Two — and yet it is wide awake. It knows. Here’s the strange part. Even though you can’t find any thing, you can’t call it nothing either — because it is knowing all of this, right now. Something in there is awake. Something is alert enough to be reading these very words and understanding them as they pass. Think of a clear mirror. A mirror holds no picture of its own — it’s “empty,” it isn’t keeping anything — and yet it’s not blank or dead. It’s perfectly clear, and so it can show a whole world exactly as it is. Your awareness is like that: empty of any fixed thing, but clear, awake, alive, always knowing. People sometimes call this clear, knowing quality the “light” of the mind — and here I have to be careful with you, because that word fools almost everyone. There is no actual light. It is not a glow you could ever see. If you go hunting behind your eyes for some inner brightness, you’ve simply turned awareness into one more thing you’re aware of — and we already said it can never be a thing. “Light” is just the word we reach for because knowing feels lucid and awake, the way a clear morning feels clear. It points at a feeling, not a lamp.

Three — and underneath, there’s a joy that doesn’t come from anywhere. This is the part I most want you to find for yourself, because it’s the part that changes everything. Think about how happiness usually works. You’re happy because of something — a gift, a friend, a good grade, a win. Which means the moment the thing goes away, the happy goes with it. That kind of joy is always on loan; you’re always about to have to give it back. But there’s another kind. Have you ever, just for a second, felt glad to be alive for no reason at all? Nothing happened — just a quiet, easy gladness, a small yes somewhere in you. Most people get tiny flashes of it and never even notice. That gladness isn’t arriving from anything outside you. It’s the gladness of awareness itself — not gladness about anything, just the light, easy feeling of being awake at all. (Notice that word light again — I don’t mean a glow. I mean light the way a weight lifting off you feels light.) And here’s the world-changing part: because nothing causes it, nothing can take it away. You can lose the gift, flunk the grade, lose the game — and that quiet gladness is still there underneath, the way the sky is still there behind the clouds. It never went anywhere. The clouds just hid it for a while.

So the watcher you went looking for is not a cold, empty quiet. It is empty, wide awake, and quietly joyful — all at once, all one thing. Empty the way open sky is empty. Awake the way a clear, rested mind is awake. Joyful the way you feel when, for no reason at all, you’re simply glad to be here. Not one of those three is a sight or a sound or a thing you could point to — they’re the texture of awareness itself, which is exactly why no word, not even “light,” ever quite lands on it. The words are only fingers pointing at the moon. Don’t stare at the finger.

That is the nature of your mind. That’s what all the old books, in all the old languages, keep circling around and pointing at. And now you know what I meant back in Chapter 1: once you’ve truly met it — even for one second — you can’t unmeet it. You will know, for the rest of your life, that beneath every single bad day there is a quiet gladness that nothing can switch off.

Why This Is the Secret Engine of Everything

Once you’ve felt even a flicker of that, the way you hold your whole life loosens.

Here’s how I think about it as a grown-up. All the outside stuff — the labels, the grades, the followers, the things people swear are “important” — is on loan, exactly like borrowed happiness. It’s a bunch of symbols, and symbols can flip in a minute. I won’t lie and say seeing this makes life perfect: I can’t snap my fingers and put a million dollars in my bank account and retire just because I understand it. But what does happen is that I can look at things clearly. My feelings stop bossing my judgment around. The light underneath doesn’t flicker just because the symbols changed.

And notice — the calm that comes from this isn’t me gritting my teeth and forcing myself not to care. It grows on its own, out of understanding. When you really see how something works — that it was always on loan, that it was never the light — your grip on it just relaxes. You don’t have to pry your fingers open one by one. They open by themselves.

That’s the engine of this whole book. Everything else — the breathing, the pictures, the sitting still — is here to walk you back, again and again, to that empty-bright-warm watcher, until “Who is seeing?” stops being a riddle and becomes the most familiar friend you’ll ever have.

Questions to talk about

No answer key. These are for arguing over, with someone who read the same chapter.

  1. Look at your hand. You can see it, so it is a thing you see, not the seer. Follow that with a sound, then a thought. What is left over when you have ruled out everything you can notice?
  2. Most books stop at finding the watcher. This one asks what the watcher is like, and says the answer surprises nearly everyone. Before reading on, what did you expect pure awareness to feel like?
  3. 'You don't have to pry your fingers open one by one. They open by themselves.' What is he saying about the difference between letting go and forcing yourself to let go?
